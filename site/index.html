◆ MUTAMBO RESEARCH Zambian economy · in data 01 / 08

Zambia,
in beef.

The country is chasing its first billion dollars of beef exports. We asked a bigger question: what would ten billion look like?

Compiled from the public record. Every figure sourced, every chart built from the numbers up. Where a number is an estimate, we say so.

01THE STARTING LINE

A big herd that barely sells

Zambia has five million cattle and, until October 2025, exported almost none of them. The herd is savings on the hoof, not a business — yet.

The government wants to change that: a target of at least US$1 billion in beef exports a year by around 2031. The first-ever formal consignment — 55 head from Namwala, killed at Kalomo — crossed into the DRC in October 2025.1,2 That's the on-ramp. This report is about the road far past it.

02THE BIG NUMBER

Ten billion is a lot of cows

Work backwards from the dollars and the animals appear. $10B of beef is roughly 1.7 million tonnes — and about 46 million head standing behind it.

At ~US$6,000 a tonne for export beef,5,6 $10B is ~1.67 million tonnes. At ~180 kg of saleable beef per animal, that's ~9.3 million head killed for export every year. A herd can only be slaughtered as fast as it breeds — so at a world-class 28% off-take, the standing herd needed is about 46 million cattle. Nine times today's.

03THE GEOGRAPHY

The cows are already by the tracks

Half the national herd sits in a handful of Southern Province districts — strung along the exact line the main railway already runs.

04THE RAILS

Rails built for copper can carry beef

Zambia's railways were laid to haul minerals out and fuel in. The lucky part: the line already threads cattle country — the fix is temperature, not track.

TAZARA → the Gulf

Kapiri Mposhi to Dar es Salaam. A US$1.4B rebuild (signed 2025) targets 2.4 million tonnes a year and opens the Gulf's huge halal market.10,11

Lobito → Europe

The Benguela line to Angola's Atlantic coast — the short route to the EU and the Americas, with a planned spur into Zambia.12

05THE COLD CHAIN

Cold is the whole game

Beef is perishable. Break the temperature once and you don't delay the product — you condemn it. That single fact designs the logistics.

Live animals move from grazing districts to rail-served export abattoirs; carcasses are blast-frozen and chilled at hubs; reefer containers ride rail and sea to port cold stores at Dar es Salaam and Lobito, with air-freight from Lusaka for the priciest chilled cuts.

06THE POWER PROBLEM

None of it works without power

A cold chain needs electricity that never blinks. Zambia's grid — 82% hydro — spent 2024 in 21-hour blackouts as Lake Kariba ran dry.

When the drought cut Kariba to ~8% of usable storage, generation fell to ~900 MW against ~2,400 MW of demand.13,14 For minerals an outage is lost output; for beef it can be lost product. So the chain can't ride on the grid — each hub needs firm, on-site solar + battery. The load is small; the reliability is everything.

07THE MONEY

Who pays for all this

Roughly US$15–25 billion over 25–30 years — and most of it is biological, not concrete.

The single biggest line is the herd itself: restocking, genetics, pasture, water, and the vet care that cuts mortality. The market-access gate — national traceability and foot-and-mouth zoning, on the Botswana/Namibia model15,16 — is cheap by comparison but absolutely required. No traceability, no premium markets.

08THE CLIMB

A thirty-year climb

The road to $10B runs through the $1B target, not around it — the same heartland, rails and herd, scaled in stages.

PHASE 1
→ $1B
2026–2031 · Prove it
  • Concentrate on the Southern heartland, on the existing spine
  • Disease-free compartment + traceability pilot
  • 2–3 EU/halal abattoirs; TAZARA reefer to Dar
PHASE 2
→ $4–5B
2031–2040 · Scale the herd
  • Herd to ~15–20M via genetics & finishing
  • Western Province in behind vaccination
  • Namwala rail spur; Lobito spur online
PHASE 3
→ $10B
2040–2055 · Top-ten exporter
  • Herd ~40–46M; off-take near 28%
  • Both oceans at full reefer capacity
  • A premium "Zambian grass-fed" brand
