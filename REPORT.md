# Zambia at $10B Beef — Research & Scenario Brief

*What does a $10 billion beef export industry mean for Zambia?*

An independent research and scenario brief. The $10B figure is an **analytical
anchor** used to expose the system-level constraints — herd, rails, cold chain,
energy, and finance — that lie beyond the government's stated $1B target.
It is not a forecast, a government plan, or investment advice. Figures marked
*illustrative* are order-of-magnitude estimates built on the cited data and
standard livestock/logistics assumptions.

## 1. From a slogan to a scale

- Zambia targets **≥ US$1B in beef exports/year by ~2031**. [MFL]
- First formal consignment: **55 head from Namwala, slaughtered at Kalomo**,
  exported to the DRC in **October 2025**. ~20 countries have expressed interest. [APA / MFL]
- A $1B industry is a *commercial upgrade* of what Zambia already has.
  A $10B industry is a **national transformation over 25–30 years**.

## 2. Current position (2024/25)

| Indicator | Value |
|---|---|
| National cattle herd | **~5.1 million** (5,115,495) |
| Held in traditional / smallholder sector | **84%** (93.5% smallholder) |
| Est. commercial off-take rate | **~5–8%** (median 2 head/household/yr) |
| Calf mortality (traditional) | **20–35%**; adult ~9% |
| Calving rate | **45–50%** |
| Largest processor (Zambeef) | ~60,000 head/yr, 8 abattoirs |

The herd is large but held mainly for draft power, milk and savings — not meat.

## 3. The arithmetic of $10 billion (central scenario)

Working backwards from the dollar figure:

| Step | Assumption | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Export earnings | — | **$10.0B / yr** |
| Realised price | ~$6,000 / tonne (deboned export beef) | — |
| Export beef required | $10B ÷ $6,000 | **~1.67 million tonnes / yr** |
| Saleable beef per head | 180 kg (≈2× today's smallholder carcass) | — |
| Head slaughtered for export | 1.67 Mt ÷ 180 kg | **~9.3 million head / yr** |
| + domestic/regional (×1.4) | — | **~13 million head / yr** total slaughter |
| Sustainable off-take | 28% | — |
| **National herd implied** | 13M ÷ 0.28 | **~46 million cattle (~9× today)** |

**Company this puts Zambia in:** Australia earns ~$10B from beef with ~28M head;
Argentina runs ~53M head. A $10B Zambian sector implies a herd it has never had —
reached only by decades of raising off-take, cutting mortality, and roughly
doubling carcass weight. The live model (sliders) is on the deployed page.

Benchmark prices: global average frozen beef ~US$4,737/t (2024); Australian 90CL
record ~US$6,412/t (Feb 2025); Mercosur steer index ~US$4.83/kg CW.

## 4. Where the cattle already are

Leading districts (2024, thousands of head) — **all in Southern Province**:

- Monze **285** · Itezhi-Tezhi **284** · Namwala **184** · Kalomo **176** (Choma also top-tier)
- Leading Southern districts ≈ **49% of the national herd**.
- Western Province (Barotseland) holds a large traditional herd but sits **off the
  rail grid** and inside Zambia's oldest **foot-and-mouth (FMD) zone** — a
  market-access problem before a logistics one.

**The geography is a gift:** Monze, Mazabuka and Choma lie *directly on* the
Zambia Railways spine; Namwala/Itezhi-Tezhi/Kalomo are a short feeder away.

## 5. Rails built for copper — bent toward beef

The spine (~846 km, 1,067 mm gauge): Livingstone → Choma → Monze → Mazabuka →
Lusaka → Kabwe → Kapiri Mposhi → Ndola → Kitwe. It threads the cattle belt.
From Kapiri Mposhi, two ocean gates:

- **TAZARA (east)** → Dar es Salaam, ~1,860 km. **US$1.4B revitalisation** (CCECC,
  30-yr concession, signed Sept 2025): capacity **0.2 → 2.4 Mt/yr in 3 years**,
  transit −⅔, 34 locos / 760 wagons. Opens the **Gulf (halal)** and Asia.
- **Lobito Corridor (west)** → Atlantic port of Lobito via DRC, ~1,300 km + planned
  greenfield Zambia spur. First full year (2025) ~200 kt; ~1 Mt/yr target by 2030.
  Shortens the route to **Europe and the Americas** (premium markets).

**The conversion job is temperature, not track:** reefer containers on flat wagons
with yard power, livestock-loading points, and rail-served cold hubs — *not* new
trunk line, except a Namwala/feedlot spur and (later) rail into Western Province.

## 6. The cold chain (three tiers) and its locations

1. **Live haul** — grazing districts → zonal export abattoirs.
2. **Rail-served export abattoirs + cold hubs** (blast-freeze + chilled store):
   - **Kalomo** — anchor abattoir (first DRC export)
   - **Choma / Monze** — primary export hubs, heart of the herd, on the line
   - **Namwala** — feedlot cluster (short spur/road link to Choma)
   - **Lusaka / Kafue** — consolidation + KKIA air freight for premium chilled cuts
   - **Kapiri Mposhi** — reefer transload onto TAZARA (east-west pivot)
   - **Ndola / Kitwe** — Copperbelt + DRC market + Lobito interchange
   - **Mongu / Kaoma** — Western Province quarantine/vaccination gateway
3. **Corridor + port cold** — reefer containers on TAZARA & Lobito; port cold
   stores at **Dar es Salaam** and **Lobito**.

*Fleet (illustrative, $10B scale):* on the order of **~1,500 reefer road trucks**
and **several thousand reefer containers** cycling farm → hub → port, plus a large
live-haul fleet.

## 7. The binding constraint: energy

- Zambia's grid is **82% hydro** and has been in crisis: 2024 drought dropped Lake
  Kariba live storage to **~7.7%** of usable capacity; ZESCO load-shedding reached
  **up to 21 hours/day**; generation ~900 MW vs ~2,400 MW demand (~750 MW deficit).
- For minerals an outage is lost output; **for beef an outage can be lost product**.
- The chain must not ride on Kariba. Each hub needs **firm, mostly on-site power:
  solar PV + battery storage + grid + captive backup**. Zambia's ~5–6 kWh/m²/day
  insolation makes this natural (cf. Chisamba solar; a 1,000 MW solar PPA).
- *Illustrative* dedicated firm capacity for the full chain: **~150–250 MW** —
  small beside national demand, but it must be reliable.

## 8. What it costs, and who pays (illustrative capital stack, ~$20B)

| Component | Illustrative capital |
|---|---|
| Herd · pasture · water (genetics, AI, dams, dip tanks, extension) | **~$9B** |
| Processing + cold hubs (EU/halal abattoirs, blast freeze) | ~$3B |
| Logistics (reefer road fleet, rail reefer, port cold stores) | ~$3B |
| Disease control + traceability (LITS, FMD zoning, vet capacity) | ~$1.8B |
| Energy (solar + storage at hubs) | ~$1.6B |
| Rail spurs & reefer terminals | ~$1.6B |
| **Total** | **≈ $20B over 25–30 years** |

The **herd dominates the bill** and compounds slowly (biological capital).
Traceability is comparatively cheap but the **market-access gate** — no
traceability, no premium markets (the Botswana/Namibia EU model).

*Financing:* blended finance — DFIs (IFC, AfDB, US DFC via Lobito, DBSA), China via
TAZARA, sovereign guarantees, livestock/warehouse receipts, out-grower &
contract-farming finance, agri- & diaspora bonds, and carbon co-finance from
rangeland restoration and methane reduction.

## 9. A three-phase path (through $1B, not around it)

- **Phase 1 — → $1B (2026–2031, "prove it"):** consolidate the Southern Province
  heartland on the existing spine; disease-free compartment/zone + traceability
  pilot; 2–3 EU/halal abattoirs (Kalomo, Choma, Monze); seed reefer fleet; TAZARA
  reefer to Dar for Gulf/halal; grow DRC volume via road + early Lobito.
- **Phase 2 — → $4–5B (2031–2040, "scale the herd"):** herd to ~15–20M; extend
  disease zoning, bring Western Province in behind vaccination; Namwala/feedlot
  rail spurs; Lobito Zambia spur online; port cold stores at scale.
- **Phase 3 — → $10B (2040–2055, "top-ten exporter"):** herd ~40–46M; national
  traceability; off-take near 28%; both ocean gates at full reefer capacity;
  premium "Zambian grass-fed" brand into EU, Gulf, Asia, Americas — beef becomes a
  second export pillar beside copper.
